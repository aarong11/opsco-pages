Synthetic run · four worlds · five distribution points each

What an undisclosed arrangement looks like

Four seeded worlds run through the real custody, ceremony and consumables modules. One property differs in each, so any difference in the glass is attributable to that property rather than to whoever was on shift.

Reading the glass

Marks and what they mean

A pane is a deterministic function of one plate, so the same queue state always draws the same picture and any change to the state changes it.

colour

An ink may only be used while the stock ledger accounts for it. An ink at zero drops out and its cells go to ground — the pane reads cold because the stores were empty, not because a designer chose grey.

came

The lead between cells is a separation that held. Where two cells run together behind a dashed red join, the separation collapsed: one controller behind two roles that were meant to be independent.

stones

One along the top edge per signed allocation in that plate.

wedges

A tally along the foot, one per withheld item. Refusals are recorded as fully as allocations — a queue documented only by what it did has no record of what it declined.

ribbon

Wait order against allocation order. Vertical is healthy. Every leaning line is a place allocation left the queue order.

Signatures

What each one is read from, and what it is not

All three are computed from declared, structural facts the system already holds — controller identity, the conflict register, allocation order. None is an inference about how anyone spoke, wrote, or behaved.

custody

Fog formation

shared controller behind unrelated names

Read from the controller field on each role assignment. Refused at appointment, because afterwards the choice is between an unverified result and doing the work again.

not: Names. Three companies with different letterheads are one party if one hand appoints all three, and no amount of name-checking finds that.
ceremonies

Out-of-band alliance

sustained co-absence with no registered edge

Counted over days from the attendance record: who is never co-present, and is there an edge in the register that accounts for it.

not: A conclusion. Co-absence is a prompt to check whether the register is complete — people avoid each other for good reasons, and most of them are nobody's business.
dispatch

Back-channel timing

allocation order diverging from wait order

Both ranks are recorded on every allocation. Divergence is arithmetic, not inference, and the healthy value is exactly zero.

not: A behavioural sniffer. Nothing here reads latency, phrasing, or tone. The queue exposes no steerable variable; that is a structural property, not a detection.
Verification by residue

A ceremony is verified by what it consumed

An attendance record is a claim written by the same process that scheduled the work. It costs nothing to write and nothing to write falsely. Production leaves residue: ink leaves a lot, paper leaves a shelf, a meter advances, and stock has to be replaced afterwards — each one a movement somebody physically made against a purchase order and a named supplier.

The third card is the one to care about. Production on unaccounted stock means output exists that the books cannot explain, and the gap is measured in sheets rather than inferred from anyone’s behaviour. The second is quieter and more common: stock drawn with no production behind it.

Meters are attributed by the spec a printer holds, never by the room it stands in. A device holding one client’s mix did not produce another’s work, and counting the whole room would charge one client’s output to another’s session — the exact confusion this exists to remove. A printer holding no spec that advances is its own finding: production attributable to no session at all.

The cost curve

Why an expanding book costs more per client, not less

Brand specs do not share. Each client’s approved mix is its own, and a printer holding it cannot produce another client’s work without a purge that is itself a recorded, costed event. So the book does not spread fixed costs over more work — it multiplies them: another set of lots, another device, another maintenance contract.

That expense is also a control. Consumable and maintenance cost per client has to keep pace with that client’s output. Where output grows and cost does not, the work was produced on somebody else’s stock — which is why CLIENT-D, sitting well under the median, is a question about the stock records rather than about anyone’s conduct.

The screen is deliberately blunt: a median, a tolerance, a direction. It says where to go and look. It does not say what you will find, and it never produces a finding about a person.

Standing back

What all of these checks have in common

What this run is not evidence of

These are invented worlds. A signature appearing in one of them says the detector fires on the shape; it says nothing about whether the shape occurs, how often, or what it means when it does. Base rates come from live books, and none were used here.

A signature is a prompt to check the register, never a finding about a person. Fog formation asks whether the controller field is complete; co-absence asks whether the conflict register is; divergence asks whether the allocation code still does what it says. All three questions are about our records rather than about anyone's conduct — which is what keeps them answerable.
