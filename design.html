Design guide

Ink on dark glass, and what each colour may mean

The palette is not decoration here — it is a small vocabulary with fixed meanings, so a reader can tell from colour alone whether something completed, was refused, or is about access.

Palette

Three inks and three neutrals

Semantic colour is separate from the accent. Relief and delivery carry meaning and are never used to differentiate one series from another; separation doubles as the accent because access is the subject of most of what we build.

Relief
#D14257 · --relief

Refusals, held state, and the relief ceremony. Never used decoratively: a red mark means something was declined or someone was covered.

Delivery
#4CA87A · --delivery

Passes, delivery events, vesting. The only colour permitted to mean “this completed”.

Separation
#6E82E8 · --separation

The accent, and the separation ink. Structural emphasis and anything about access.

Came
#262E37 · --came

Every hairline, border and rule. Named after the lead between panes because that is what it does — it holds pieces apart.

Ground
#0D1014 · --ground

The page ground. Blue-black rather than neutral, so the inks sit on it as glass rather than as UI.

Bone
#E8E3D9 · --ink

Primary text. Warm off-white; pure white reads as glare against the inks.

Colour is never the only carrier of meaning. A refused item is labelled refused and carries its evidence reference; the red is confirmation, not information. That rule exists for colour-vision accessibility and for printing, and it also survives being screenshotted into a document with the colour stripped.

Typography

Two faces, one scale

Spectral for prose, IBM Plex Mono for anything a machine produced — identifiers, hashes, counts, console output, labels. If it came out of the system it is set in mono, so the reader always knows which side of the boundary a string is from.

--step5Page title
--step3Section heading
--step2Standfirst
--step1Body copy, set near 65 characters
--step0Secondary and table text
--step-1Eyebrow / label

Numbers that line up in a column take tabular-nums without exception. Counts, hashes, hours and money are compared by eye far more often than they are read, and proportional digits make that comparison unreliable.

Structure

Devices that encode something true

  • Number a sequence, never a list

    Numbered markers appear where order carries information — the eight rehearsal scenarios, a process with steps. A grid of four capabilities is not a sequence and is not numbered.

  • Hairlines are came, not decoration

    A rule between two things means they are separate things. Grids are one-pixel gaps over the came colour so the separation is structural rather than drawn.

  • Wide content scrolls inside itself

    Tables, hashes and console output get their own scroll container. The page body never scrolls sideways.

Writing

How a refusal is worded

Most of what this system says to people is “no”. Getting that wording right matters more than any visual decision on this page.

  • Name the control and the evidence reference

    “Refused: registered conflict, evidence EV-2291.” The reader can act on that, and can contest it.

  • Never state the reason

    Not why the conflict exists, not who registered it, not anything about the other party. A refusal that leaks the reason turns a control into a disclosure channel and cannot be shown to both sides.

  • Say what would change the answer

    A refusal without a route forward reads as arbitrary and generates an appeal instead of an action.

  • No apology, no hedging

    The control did its job. “Unfortunately we are unable to…” invites an argument about whether it should have.
