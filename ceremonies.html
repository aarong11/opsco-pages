Ceremonies

Named interactions, and what each one refuses

A ceremony is an interaction the system recognises by name, evaluates against a published rule, and records. Each is defined as much by what it declines as by what it permits.

Reading these

Guarantee, then refusal

The refusal line is the more useful half. A control that never declines anything is not a control, and the shape of what it declines is what tells you whether it is pointed at the right thing.

Dispatch offer dispatch

A ticket is offered to a worker for a window.

guarantees The window is admissible across run time and scratch tail; the worker holds no registered conflict against the work; rest periods and queue windows are satisfied.
refuses Offering by wait order only. There is no self-selection, so there is no variable a participant could steer to signal through the queue.
Custody triad custody

Three parties hold separate roles over one asset.

guarantees Separation is evaluated on common control, not on names. Three differently-named parties under one controller are one party.
refuses A triad that was clean at dispatch and is not at delivery. Controller changes are re-evaluated, not assumed stable.
Corroborated watch watch

Availability, held with an independent second watcher.

guarantees Pay follows the part of the window a partner actually overlapped — not the part they were assigned. Agents count as watchers and face the same independence test.
refuses Paying per observation. A register whose contributors are paid to find things fills up with things; a quiet watch is a complete watch.
Independent invention invention

A contribution combines only against a blinded replication.

guarantees The certificate is the replication. A contribution with no matching independent result does not combine and does not vest.
refuses Provenance by assertion. 'Worked with', 'was linked to' and 'contributed' are three different claims and the system will not let the third read as the first.
Conflict-aware invitation ceremonies

A shared ceremony is convened with a guest list.

guarantees An invitation that would place two parties to a registered conflict in one room is refused, and the refusal names the evidence reference.
refuses Disclosing why. The refusal never states the reason, who registered it, or anything about the other party — that is what makes it usable in front of both.
Ink and pane consumables

A ceremony state is rendered as a checksum pane.

guarantees A colour may only be used where the ledger accounts for the ink. The budget is derived from stock movements and cannot be supplied by hand.
refuses Decorative colour. A pane that could show a hue the stores cannot account for would forge exactly the provenance it exists to prove.
Worked example

Why the watch pays eight hours, not ten

Two watchers cover an eight-hour window. A works 08:00–12:00, B works 10:00–16:00. Between them they log ten watcher-hours, and the window is corroborated for eight of them — from 08:00, when A starts with B not yet on, there is no independent second watcher until 10:00.

The payable period is the union of the intervals where an independent partner was actually on. It is not the sum of the hours worked, and it is not the hours assigned. A partner who logs off early ends the payable period early for everyone on that watch.

That last consequence is a real exposure and it points the wrong way: a worker may still be on when pay stops, because a third party logged off. Availability that is unpaid for that reason is still likely working time. The system surfaces an uncorroborated watch while it is open so it can be closed or re-partnered in the moment — whether the uncorroborated remainder must be paid anyway is a question for counsel, not for the scheduler.
