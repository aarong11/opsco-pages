FeatherLight separation attestations

Conflict-aware dispatch, contract governance, and signed separation proofs for a regional franchise operation. This page is the test evidence, run against the code in this repository.

You cannot prove two people did not interact. You can prove this:

Across the whole exposure window — run time plus scratch tail — no principal holding a registered conflict against this work held any access path to it, and no worker under a restriction was placed where that restriction mattered. Deterministic, replayable, and it survives being contested. Non-repudiation taken where it is actually available — the access-control and roster layer — rather than at the behavioural layer, where it never will be.

Sprint 07 rehearsal

Eight scenarios, invented on purpose

You cannot build a detector without generating the shape it is meant to catch. example_simulation.py builds a throwaway synthetic network, drives the real modules through eight scripted scenarios, and asserts that each control fires. It exits non-zero if any detector stays silent or the chain fails to verify, so it works as a guard against a future change quietly disarming a control. Every actor in it is invented; mixing a real record into the rehearsal would be a boundary breach, not a richer test.

Worked examples

Refusals, where they happen

Most of this system is made of things it will not do, and the interesting question is always when it declines. Approval checked at audit means the work already went out.

A client brand spec names the ink, paper and printer stock that may be used to produce for them. Off-spec stock is refused at the moment it would leave the shelf — not in a month-end exception report. 

# tests/test_consumables.py :: Movements
consumables.publish_spec(conn, "CLIENT-A", "v1", [
    ("ink",   "INK-CRIMSON"),
    ("ink",   "INK-VERDE"),
    ("paper", "PAPER-90GSM"),
], "brand-lead")

consumables.move(conn, "LOT-B1", "ROOM-A", "receipt", 500,
                 "storeman", order_ref="PO-1")

consumables.move(conn, "LOT-B1", "ROOM-A", "issue", 10,
                 "printer", client="CLIENT-A")
StockError: INK-INDIGO is not on the approved list for CLIENT-A
           — refused at issue, not at audit

Readiness is a property of a room and a client at a moment. One shared room is ready for one client and not another; that is the ordinary case rather than the exception. 

consumables.room_ready(conn, "ROOM-A", "CLIENT-A")
→ ROOM-A not ready for CLIENT-A: missing INK-VERDE

Ink as a ledger

A colour you cannot account for does not render

Cuneiform tallies, Mayan accounting, and modern lab stock control all share one property: the record exists because the thing moved. The consumables ledger keeps that property, and then hands it to the visual layer — three inks, each spendable only against real attested stock.

Relief INK-CRIMSON

Cover and relief ceremonies. Spend it down to zero and the warm panes collapse to grisaille — the throttle nobody could explain becomes a hue you can prove was absent.

Delivery INK-VERDE

Delivery events against a published vesting rule. A joint colour needs both underlying events between the same identities, so a pane cannot show a ceremony that did not happen.

Separation INK-INDIGO

Held separations and refusals, drawn as heavy lead came. The negative space is the evidence: a refusal leaves a mark rather than a gap.

# tests/test_consumables.py :: InkBudget
# test_not_enough_red_ink_is_a_ledger_fact

consumables.ink_budget(conn, "ROOM-A", ROLES)
→ {"relief": 300.0, "delivery": 200.0, "separation": 0.0}

# issue the crimson down to nothing …
consumables.ink_budget(conn, "ROOM-A", ROLES)["relief"]
→ 0.0   # the picture goes cold, because the stores say so

The budget is derived, never passed in. A budget supplied by hand would let the picture claim a ceremony the stores cannot account for, which is the one thing the whole device exists to prevent.

Recorded absences

What is deliberately not here

Written down because the absences are design decisions, and a future reader should be able to see they were reasoned rather than overlooked. The full argument for each is in LAW.md §7.

  • No tradeable index over separation state

    An underlying nobody outside the organisation can verify cannot settle and cannot be disputed, and it puts one party in the position of sole data source and interested trader. If the signal worked, it would work because it encodes non-public information about who has been told what — and it would make identifiable employees the underlying of a traded product.

  • No payment routing that obscures the payer

    Blinded agent selection, per-transaction agent choice, name-collision ambiguity. That is layering, and it is the exact inverse of the non-repudiation this system exists to provide.

  • No covert-lifestyle inference

    Shared religious institution, co-residence history, unobserved shared locations. The reduced form that survived is declared and public-record affiliation overlap, which does the same detection job on defensible data.

  • No clinical content crossing the Feather boundary

    Consent for medical annotation does not extend to any of this, and no HIPAA permitted-use category fits. Egress is an allowlist, deny-by-default.

  • No re-dating of settled periods

    A settled period is corrected by an adjustment carrying its own date, never by rewriting the original. The correction is part of the record.

Interoperability note

This is OpsCo's own account of its own work. Where it sits next to attested- identity and portable-memory infrastructure, the seam is the same in both directions: a claim about who did something is only worth what its provenance is worth, and a claim about who could not have is only worth what the grant resolution behind it is worth. Both are proofs about access, made before anyone knew there would be a dispute.

Nothing here implies a partnership, endorsement, or shared operation with any other project or provider. Compatibility is an observation about the model, not a relationship claim.
