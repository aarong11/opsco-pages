OpsCo · operational controls · onboarding

Proof about access, not about people

We run controls over shared compute and shared premises: who may do which work, in which window, and what evidence exists afterwards. This site is the public account of how that works.

The claim

What we will and will not say

You cannot prove two people did not interact. You can prove this:

Across the whole exposure window — run time plus scratch tail — no principal holding a registered conflict against this work held any access path to it, and no worker under a restriction was placed where that restriction mattered. Here are the grant paths, the evidence refs, the policy version, and a signature.

Non-repudiation taken where it is actually available — the access-control and roster layer — rather than at the behavioural layer, where it never will be.

What we do

Four things, and they share one spine

Every one of them ends in a signed, replayable record written before anyone knew there would be a dispute. That ordering is the entire product.

01

Conflict-aware scheduling

Whether a job may run in a window, given registered conflicts and effective access grants. Nominal roles are not the input — resolved grants are, including nested groups, standing exceptions and break-glass.

emits a signed attestation per decision
02

Separation attestation

Append-only, hash-chained, Ed25519-signed decision log, replayable against a pinned policy version. Built to survive being contested two years later.

verify without our cooperation
03

Metering and settlement

Consumption tagged at submission, allocated monthly, reconciled against the invoice. Consumables the same way: balances derived from movements, never set.

open books — every member sees the same figures
04

Re-identification risk

Residual identifier detection, quasi-identifier scoring, linkage detection and deduplication. Runs inside the controller's boundary; only scores and remediation tickets leave.

How work reaches you

One queue, two tracks

Work arrives as a ticket priced from a published rate card by (code, skill, band). The same rate applies to everyone on the ticket. There is no discretionary component and no negotiated rate.

rehearsal

Synthetic, graded, paid

Cases generated from the same task families the live queue emits, with ground truth held separately. Graded blind, against a rubric published to you before you start.

paid at the same band rate as live work
live

Real work, four eyes

Offered only where you are qualified for that task family. Qualification is mechanical and published: a count, a rolling median, no unresolved flags, a named signature, expiring annually.

first 20 tickets carry a second signature

Rehearsal is paid at the same rate as live. A discounted training track is an inducement to accept a worse rate, and it gives the organisation a standing reason to keep people in rehearsal. The graded corpus has value — it is what lets us show that our auditors are calibrated.

Refusal is a correct answer. On a graded case whose honest conclusion is “this cannot be assessed on the evidence provided”, that answer scores full marks. A rubric that only rewards findings trains people to find things.

What we decline

Recorded absences

Written down because they are design decisions rather than gaps. If you are asked for any of these, the answer is already on this page and you can point at it.

  • No tradeable instrument over separation state

    An underlying nobody outside the organisation can verify cannot settle and cannot be disputed, and it would make identifiable people the underlying of a traded product.

  • No payment routing that obscures the payer

    Blinded agent selection and name-collision ambiguity are layering, and the exact inverse of the non-repudiation we exist to provide.

  • No behavioural leak detection on staff

    No scoring of response latency, register, or terminology drift. The base rate is poor and the false positives land on real careers.

  • No covert-lifestyle inference

    What survives is declared and public-record affiliation overlap, which does the same detection job on defensible data.

  • No clinical content crossing the annotation boundary

    Consent for medical annotation does not extend to operational analysis. Egress is an allowlist, deny-by-default.
